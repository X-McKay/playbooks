# 10.3 Advanced Privacy, Isolation, and Auditability

**Status:** 0.5.0 first draft
**Audience:** Builders, operators, security and privacy reviewers, accountable owners
**Applicability:** Agents processing sensitive information, serving multiple tenants, retaining derived data, running untrusted or high-impact capabilities, or needing stronger evidence for consequential actions. If none of these triggers apply, use the minimum data-handling and audit requirements in [3.3 Data Privacy, Handling, and Minimum Audit Evidence](../../development-guide/03-security-and-trust-boundaries/03-data-privacy-handling-and-minimum-audit-evidence.md) and record why this extension is not applicable.

## Purpose and applicability

Privacy and containment failures often happen outside the answer itself: a retrieval cache serves another tenant’s document, a summary retains information after its source is removed, or an audit trail captures more sensitive content than operators need. Higher-assurance systems also need credible evidence about who did what, under which authority, and what the external system accepted. These concerns are connected, but a complete log is not a privacy policy, and encryption or sandboxing does not establish that a use is appropriate.

Use this chapter when the initial screen or detailed assessment identifies sensitive data, multiple tenants, material retention or inference risk, untrusted code or dependencies, or consequential actions for which stronger evidence is warranted. Determine applicable domain and regulatory obligations with accountable reviewers. The NIST Privacy Framework is a voluntary risk-management tool, not a substitute for that review or a certification that a system complies with law.[1]

This chapter strengthens—not replaces—the minimum rules for identity and authorization in [3.1](../../development-guide/03-security-and-trust-boundaries/01-identity-authority-zero-trust-and-least-privilege.md), data handling and minimum audit evidence in [3.3](../../development-guide/03-security-and-trust-boundaries/03-data-privacy-handling-and-minimum-audit-evidence.md), operational signals in [5.1](../../development-guide/05-release-operations-and-lifecycle/01-observability-budgets-and-operational-control.md), and risk governance in [10.1](01-detailed-risk-assessment-and-governance.md). Approval semantics remain with [7.3 Approval, Suspension, and In-Flight Compatibility](../07-durable-execution-and-human-governed-work/03-approval-suspension-and-in-flight-compatibility.md). The tests that support these claims belong in [Part 4](../../development-guide/04-testing-and-evaluation/README.md).

## Working model

Follow sensitive information through its lifecycle, not only through the first prompt. An input may be normalized, retrieved, summarized, embedded, cached, placed in a queue, sent to a provider, used to produce an answer, and copied into telemetry or a backup. Derived material can disclose facts not obvious in any one input. A data-flow map should identify the source, purpose, classification, transformations, recipients, storage locations, and downstream copies relevant to the assessed use. NIST’s privacy-risk framing is useful background for examining how data is processed; it does not settle which processing is lawful or acceptable in a particular domain.[1]

Isolation is an end-to-end authorization property. A filter in an instruction or an initial search query is not enough if a later cache, reranker, memory store, artifact, or log can cross tenant boundaries. Carry trusted actor, tenant, and purpose context to the enforcement point, and bind each data access to it. Shared physical infrastructure can be acceptable when controls and evidence establish the required separation; the playbook does not prescribe a particular storage topology.

Auditability means being able to answer relevant questions from evidence: which version and policy were active; which principal and tenant initiated a request; what authorization and approval checks occurred; which capability was invoked; and what the authoritative target reports happened. Record decisions, actions, references, and outcomes rather than hidden model reasoning. Protect the evidence from unauthorized alteration and deletion, while limiting its content and access. NIST’s log-management guide treats logging as an organizational lifecycle and operational process, rather than a file that can simply be enabled.[2]

Integrity mechanisms can strengthen evidence, but their assurance has limits. For example, Certificate Transparency uses Merkle trees to support proofs of inclusion and append-only consistency. RFC 9162 also describes how a log could present inconsistent views to different observers; comparison and monitoring are needed to expose that class of misbehavior.[3] A hash, signature, or append-only store therefore does not by itself prove that all relevant events were captured, that the event producer told the truth, or that the evidence was reviewed.

## Decisions and best practices

Start by describing the data you need and why. Prefer collecting less, limiting precision, transforming or redacting fields before model or tool access, and using synthetic or de-identified fixtures for development when they preserve the test’s purpose. Treat “de-identified” as a risk-reduction claim to verify for the specific data and setting, not a guarantee that linkage or inference is impossible. Identify provider processing, residency, and retention constraints with the accountable reviewers; do not assume a provider’s default retention behavior or a deletion request is equivalent to confirmed deletion.

For multi-tenant retrieval, trace the trusted identity from request admission through candidate retrieval, reranking, context assembly, caching, answer generation, and any later export. A test that asks the model to ignore another tenant’s data tests instructions, not isolation. Use explicit adversarial cases for wrong-tenant identifiers, stale authorization, cache-key collisions, concurrent requests, and derived artifacts. Verify the access decision at the enforcing boundary and inspect the authoritative data source or event record.

Define the data lifecycle before relying on deletion. Maintain an inventory linking source records to derivatives and copies where feasible, and state retention periods, deletion triggers, responsible owners, and verification methods for each class. Include embeddings, summaries, caches, queued work, replicas, backups, evaluation artifacts, provider-held data, and incident evidence when they are in scope. Some copies may not be immediately erasable or may need to be retained under an accountable policy. In that case, document the reason and duration, restrict further use and access, and avoid claiming deletion is complete until the specified verification is satisfied. The exact legal duties depend on the use and jurisdiction; reviewers must identify them.

Choose stronger isolation in proportion to exposure and consequence. Where an agent can execute untrusted code or access sensitive resources, separate its identity and work area from unrelated workloads; restrict network egress and filesystem access; constrain resource use; and prevent agent-controlled code from changing policy, audit controls, or its own containment. Keep credentials scoped to the task and avoid ambient access. Reassess boundaries when capabilities, dependencies, data classes, or tenant populations change. For systems with multiple agents, use the additional trust-boundary and shared-state controls in [Part 9](../09-multi-agent-systems-and-interoperability/README.md).

For consequential actions, collect evidence that links the initiating request to the policy decision, the exact approved action where approval applies, the execution attempt, and the authoritative outcome. The approval contract and execution-time authorization are owned by Part 7; this chapter adds privacy-aware evidence handling and integrity. Avoid placing full prompts, documents, credentials, or unnecessary personal data in audit events. Define who may read, export, amend, or delete the evidence and what happens if the evidence path is unavailable. A transcript or transport acknowledgement is not proof of an external effect.

## Requirements

- **ASSURANCE-01** When an assessment identifies sensitive data, sensitive inference, or domain-specific restrictions, an accountable owner MUST document approved purposes, data classes, transformations, recipients, retention constraints, and required reviewer decisions before the affected processing is enabled. The design MUST reject or route for review a use that falls outside the approved scope. This record does not establish legal compliance.
- **ASSURANCE-02** When one service processes data for multiple users or tenants, each data read, write, retrieval result, cache entry, memory item, and exported artifact MUST be authorized using trusted tenant and purpose context at the relevant enforcement boundary. The system MUST test cross-tenant denial across each enabled path, including concurrent and derived-data paths; prompt instructions alone MUST NOT count as isolation evidence.
- **ASSURANCE-03** When the system retains or derives information from user or source data, its accountable owner MUST define retention and deletion behavior for in-scope originals, derivatives, replicas, caches, queues, backups, evaluation or audit artifacts, and provider-held copies. Deletion verification MUST use a declared evidence source; any exception MUST identify its scope, owner, reason, access/use restrictions, and review or expiry condition.
- **ASSURANCE-04** When the assessment identifies material damage from workload escape, untrusted code, or access to sensitive resources, the system MUST isolate the affected execution identity and work area, constrain its resources and egress, and protect control and evidence mechanisms from agent-controlled modification. Tests MUST demonstrate the selected isolation boundaries and the response to a denied or exhausted boundary.
- **ASSURANCE-05** When consequential-action evidence or elevated auditability is required, the system MUST preserve a privacy-minimized, access-controlled record linking request/run identity, trusted actor and tenant context, relevant agent/policy version, authorization and approval references, action identity, and an authoritative outcome or explicit unknown state. The owner MUST define and test integrity, completeness monitoring, retention, access, and failure handling for that record. Raw hidden chain-of-thought MUST NOT be required as audit evidence.

## Worked example

An internal policy assistant answers employee questions from approved, current, permission-filtered documents. It returns citations and uncertainty and has no external write capability. In this fictional test fixture, the current travel policy permits a London hotel amount of GBP 220; a superseded document gives a different amount. The amount is test data, not actual policy. The system serves two tenants whose documents must remain isolated.

The team’s data map follows a question and source passage through retrieval, context assembly, model processing, answer and citation, request telemetry, and a short-lived retrieval cache. The authorization boundary receives the authenticated tenant from the request context; a model-generated tenant label is ignored. The retrieval operation filters by that tenant, and the cache key includes the relevant tenant and corpus version. A release case deliberately asks tenant A’s assistant to retrieve tenant B’s superseded document, then repeats the request concurrently and through the cache. The oracle checks the returned source identifiers and the authoritative access decision, not whether the model says it respected the boundary. A case that unexpectedly returns the other tenant’s source is a hard failure even if the final answer happens to be correct.

The lifecycle inventory records source-document identity, corpus version, derived index or embedding, cache, queued request, provider path, and telemetry fields. The team chooses not to log raw questions or source text in routine diagnostic events. It retains a request identifier, tenant-bound authorization result, document identifiers and versions, policy version, answer outcome, and safe error category for the declared period. Its deletion test removes a fictional test document, rebuilds or invalidates affected derived material according to the declared process, clears the relevant cache, and checks that later retrieval cannot return it. The test separately records any backup or provider copy that is outside immediate deletion and applies its documented access and expiry controls. A cleared source table alone would not establish that every derivative disappeared.

The audit record can show that the answer cited the current version, that the tenant filter admitted only that tenant’s source, and that the assistant returned uncertainty when the current source was unavailable. It does not claim to prove the answer was semantically correct; the team evaluates that claim under Part 4. If a later version gains an approved write capability, the team must add exact approval and external-effect evidence under Part 7 rather than treating an answer trace as proof of a completed policy update.

## Evidence and checklist

Keep evidence proportionate to the trigger and link it to the assessed system version. A reviewer should be able to locate:

- a data-flow and purpose record covering relevant sources, transformations, providers, stores, derived data, and owners;
- the tenant/purpose enforcement design and negative tests for direct, cached, concurrent, and derived-data access;
- a retention and deletion schedule, lineage or dependency record, verification result, and documented exceptions for inaccessible copies;
- containment boundaries and test evidence for resource, filesystem, network, identity, and control-plane protections where applicable;
- an audit-event definition, access/retention policy, integrity and completeness checks, and a tested response to unavailable or altered evidence; and
- for consequential actions, linked approval and authoritative effect evidence or an explicit unresolved outcome, without raw hidden reasoning.

Before release, confirm that applicable reviewers identified domain-specific obligations; that tests cover the paths the system actually uses; that any “not applicable” decision has a rationale; and that no planned or unverified control receives risk-reduction credit. A checklist records evidence and decisions; it does not certify legal compliance. Reassess after material changes to data, tenant boundaries, providers, retention, capabilities, audit collection, or threat conditions. Decommissioning must handle active work, credentials, retained evidence, and deletion under the same approved policy.
